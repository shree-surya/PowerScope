PowerScope

See where every watt goes.

A plug-in monitor for any appliance up to 6A. It shows voltage, current, real and apparent power, power factor, frequency and energy on its own display and on a dashboard it serves from its own WiFi. It only measures. It never switches the load.

What it measures

Everything comes from the same 200 ms burst of voltage and current samples, exactly ten mains cycles, with the DC offset removed first.

How it works

Two isolated sensors turn mains voltage and current into small signals. The ESP32 does the maths and serves the results.

  1. SenseZMPT101B reads the AC voltage. ACS712 reads the current on the live wire.
  2. ScaleA 10k and 20k divider plus a 100 nF capacitor bring each 5 V signal down to a safe 3.3 V.
  3. SampleThe ESP32 samples both for 200 ms, ten full cycles at 50 Hz.
  4. ComputeRMS, power, power factor, frequency and energy come from those samples.
  5. ShowThe 0.96 inch OLED and the dashboard update about once a second.

The build

A 6A junction-style box. The plug on the back goes to the wall, the appliance goes into the socket on the front, and a fully clear cover shows every wire inside.

  • Back: 6A plug to the wall, on a short cord.
  • Front: 6A socket, 0.96 inch OLED, touch pad and the MCB.
  • Clear cover: polycarbonate, fixed with tool-only screws. Live terminals sit under insulated covers.
  • Two zones: mains parts on one side and the low-voltage ESP32 side on the other, with a barrier between them.
  • Power for the ESP32: a sealed 5 V Hi-Link module with its own 1 A fuse, tapped before anything that could switch.
  • Earth: goes straight from plug to socket, not through any sensor.

Measuring only. PowerScope never opens the circuit. The 6A MCB protects the wiring and the appliance, and it is the only protection in this box.

PowerScope. A learning and demonstration project on 230 V mains, accurate to a few percent after calibration. Not a billing meter. Handle with care.
